## Implementation decisions I recommend

* Make RefLex ingestion source-member/edition scoped. Use the already frozen raw archive, source table, language table, bibliography and CSO conventions; do not call all RefLex material IPA or one consensus language list. A complete-source intake can keep unreviewed meanings unmapped and segments blank, with guarded primary/supplement admissions controlling accepted output. Preserve current curated namespaces and earlier evidence through an explicit migration or new reviewed namespace.
* Reuse the existing manifest/staging/native-hash/guarded-ledger machinery. Add source adapters for actual lexical macros, table fields or workbook columns, not a generic “extract every foreign-looking word” parser. Keep raw Unicode, original meaning/POS, variants, morphology, source pages/IDs and version/terms. Test drift and lost-field failures.
* Prefer explicit supplied readings when their conventions are established. Use orthographic profiles for documented systems; keep them source/lect/stage scoped and record exceptions and missing information. Profiles cannot restore unwritten tone or quantity. In mixed representations, retain both spelling and phonetic fields. Leave unresolved cases written-only.
* Review primary preferences as soon as a sufficiently broad, representative original slice supports them. Do not demand that richer original/Wiktionary evidence beat GLED on core count alone; retain earlier GLED rows and state residual coverage and information gaps. No source-name blanket inversion and no transfer of primary cognacy to new dictionary rows.
* Publish separate outcomes: new canonical languages, named varieties, new records, supported/mapped concepts, source/profile/written evidence and primary changes. A117,802-row intake is not117,802 new accepted records; related wrappers are not independent witnesses.
