# Colexification inference: observable targets, baselines and uncertainty

2026-11-01. Independent methodological proposal; no production implementation or rebuilt data. Scope: the accepted v2 method and its documented 7,366 constructions, 2,119,818 lexical records and 44,414 reported segmented edges. These are the reported robustness snapshot, not a fresh recount. The exploratory proposal below must not change lexical records, current descriptive exports or published clusters without separate review.

## Recommendation

Start with a **pre-registered, source-scoped held-out prediction pilot**, not a global Bayesian semantic network. For a small fixed list of concept pairs, predict whether a comparable held-out source scope records an eligible exact identity, using a regularized logistic model with a simple baseline, explicit coverage, and family/resource grouping. Report the target, sample, held-out log loss and calibration, uncertainty and missingness sensitivity alongside raw three-way evidence. Compare it to existing bounded-mass/family-max/resource-deletion scores as predictive features or rankings, not as probabilities. This answers a useful question about *this documented collection*: how well does information in other, separated records predict an eligible identity when both concepts are comparably represented? It does not estimate how many languages genuinely colexify a pair or why.

A model may be probabilistic without being Bayesian. A Bayesian version can express regularization and uncertainty transparently, but its posterior is conditional on its likelihood, sampling frame and prior; it cannot manufacture independent observations, recover missing contrasts or validate meanings. Information theory supplies proper predictive scores and a common unit of information, not a shortcut to cognitive universals.

## What the present method measures and where it loses information

The current observable is source/member/pronunciation/basis-scoped exact whole-form identity between distinct native form and parameter IDs after eligibility gates; positive, comparable nonmatch and unknown remain different. Written, segmented and reduced layers are distinct. Reported identity is neither established polysemy nor independent elicitation. The current family-balanced graph allocates each k-concept identity 1/binom(k,2) to each pair, takes a maximum within each family/resource block, sums family maxima and then takes the worst single-block deletion, requiring two known families and positive remaining weight. The 5,551-edge backbone and its communities are a robustness convention, not calibrated estimates; the existing report itself documents strong partition sensitivity.

This convention usefully limits a pathological clique, repeated dialects and shared resource dependence. Its choices nevertheless induce particular blind spots. A perfectly reliable ten-meaning lexical item contributes 1/45 to a particular pair while an unreliable two-meaning item contributes 1; there is no evidence that true pair reliability scales that way. A family with ten independent well-documented cases and a family with one case have the same maximum; a second block within the same family cannot increase the pre-deletion score. Deleting a single block makes a well-attested pair with 100 unrelated families all documented in that one compilation score zero, whereas two weak blocks can survive: common data transport is evidence of shared error risk, not necessarily identical underlying field evidence. Conversely distinct blocks and families can share a questionnaire, source, inheritance or contact. A maximum chooses the strongest available identity and ignores both nonmatches and measurement precision; `R` is neither an expected support nor an interval. The two-family gate excludes known isolates and unknown families from clustering by construction. Keep these diagnostics; compare alternatives rather than silently treating their induced ranking as ground truth.

## Target and observation

Let `e=(a,b)` be a fixed concept pair and `s` one accepted, source-member, variety, pronunciation and representation scope. Let `A_se=1` when both concepts have usable, separately identifiable eligible records within that same scope; `Y_se=1` if at least one permitted identical whole form is found, `Y_se=0` for a comparable sampled nonmatch, and `Y_se` undefined if `A_se=0`. Keep an explicit eligibility/sense gate and its version, native records and identifiers. Define the first estimand as

`p_e(x) = P(Y_se=1 | A_se=1, X_s=x, s in the declared source-scope sampling frame)`.

The frame and covariates must be named. A standardized summary can average predictions over a fixed, explicitly described set of eligible scope covariates, but it is still a collection-conditional standardization. It is not a random-language population prevalence. Duplicate scopes are clustered, not treated as independent samples; a variety-level positive-precedence union is useful descriptive output but changes the estimand and can inflate positive chance with source count. If models mix scopes, include source multiplicity and do not stitch the two meanings across them.

What exists now: positive, comparable nonmatch and unknown as defined in the existing pipeline, provenance, family, basis and some representation/loan metadata; actual scope eligibility must be reconstructed and audited from sealed database tables, not inferred from edge-only exports. Exact family trees, independently elicited senses, full questionnaires, lexeme identity/homophony labels, phonemic inventories and validated source-error rates are not generally present. Unknown includes missing elicitation and unavailable/misanalysed evidence. Eligibility for backbone and coverage for reported graph must be aligned explicitly; unreviewed sense mappings should not quietly become negative labels. Original broad slots and inferred expansions remain separate and must not be pseudo-replicates.

## A minimal predictive model

For a prespecified tractable set of pairs with both outcomes and adequate held-out comparable scopes, use

`Y_se | A_se=1 ~ Bernoulli(p_se)`

`logit(p_se) = alpha_e + beta' X_s + u_family(s) + v_resource(s)`.

Here `X` can initially contain representation basis and carefully audited source design/coverage summaries, not the same pair's outcome or future-derived counts. A partially pooled pair intercept and strongly regularized family/resource effects, or penalized logistic equivalents, protect sparse pairs; unknown family gets its own missing indicator, not a fictitious shared family. Grouped cross-validation must handle effects for unseen groups by integrating over their fitted population distribution, not looking up test outcomes. For a first run, a simpler pair-only smoothed rate and a coverage-only logistic model are mandatory baselines. A family-balanced rate and the current `R` can be compared as predictors after mapping/calibrating them using training data only. A predeclared null predicting the training prevalence provides an absolute baseline. Do not claim logistic coefficients isolate causation: resource and family are entangled, observation is selected, and training set covariates may be proxies for documentation.

If using Bayesian partial pooling, for illustration `alpha_e ~ Normal(mu, sigma_e)` and regularized zero-centred group effects can be fit to a bounded pilot, with weakly informative priors stated on the log-odds scale and varied in sensitivity analysis. Prior predictive checks must rule out implausibly extreme source-level predictions, posterior predictive checks compare counts by family, resource, coverage and basis, and simulation-based recovery examines sparse groups and confounding. A posterior interval describes model uncertainty for this conditional quantity; it is not a calibrated probability that a semantic connection is universal or genuine polysemy. Fixed-pair examples with complete separation require reporting prior sensitivity. A single family or resource block cannot identify its random-effect variance from the outcome itself.

## Chance, nulls and information

There is no single chance level. Three different counterfactuals ask different questions:

1. **Prediction baseline:** how much better than training prevalence or a coverage-aware baseline on held-out comparable scopes? Mean per-observation log-score improvement in bits is `mean[log2(p_model(Y)/p_baseline(Y))]`. This is out-of-sample predictive gain relative to that baseline, not automatically mutual information or information about cognition. Also report Brier score, reliability plots with counts and uncertainty, and family-weighted as well as scope-weighted metrics. Clip numerical probabilities only by a predeclared rule.
2. **Incidence null:** within comparable source scopes, randomize concept assignments on a bipartite identity-group/concept incidence graph using degree-preserving swaps, preserving group sizes, concept incidence degrees and scope boundaries. Exclude forbidden or ineligible incidence, preserve feasible availability, report mixing, autocorrelation and swap failures. This tests a conditional exchangeability hypothesis of assignment given those margins; it is not a phonological accident model. Restrictions and structural zeros can make the swap space disconnected; test tiny cases by exact enumeration, use multiple chains and withhold inferential labels if mixing cannot be established. Family/contact/resource dependencies are not solved by within-scope swaps. Apply the same statistic and selection procedure to each randomization, including tested pair universe and any filtering. For B sampled nulls use `(1 + number(T_b >= T_obs))/(B+1)`, with seed, Monte Carlo uncertainty and multiplicity correction across the *predefined* pair family; limited B restricts achievable adjusted significance. Treat as a model check until exchangeability and error control are validated.
3. **Form-collision null:** compare actual whole forms against source- and variety-specific distributions conditional on length, phonotactics, morphology, transcription and retained tone/quantity. For independent draws from a known form distribution, chance identity is `sum_w P_a(w) P_b(w)`; assuming a common distribution gives `sum_w P(w)^2`. This is illustrative, not empirically justified by treating a dictionary's repeated form tokens as independent draws. Length, restrictions on syllables and coarse transcriptions alter collision rates dramatically. Estimate only in sufficiently rich, consistent documented inventories; use held-out types, uncertainty and simulations. Compare measured homophony with human-reviewed homonym/polysemy distinctions and account for synonyms and bound forms. A reduced representation can collapse true contrasts, so its collision rate is distinct and cannot be 'corrected' by normalization. These nulls condition on different properties and must not be presented interchangeably.

For a toy collision, ten equiprobable forms give probability 0.1, while 100 equiprobable forms give 0.01; identity therefore need not mean the same thing across inventories. For a coverage toy, two of two comparable scopes are positive versus ten of 100: 100% versus 10% describes observations, but neither yields the prevalence in 1,000 undocumented languages. Even 2/2 gains only limited information under a well-specified binomial model: with independent exchangeable observations and a Beta(1,1) prior the posterior is Beta(3,1), assumptions which related or copied sources violate. A posterior narrow only because 100 copied dictionaries were counted independently is false precision.

Pointwise mutual information of observed binary concept incidence is not a lexical semantic truth score: it is unstable for rare pairs, depends on the reference distribution and availability, and pair selection can bias it upward. Entropy of an edge probability, `-p log2 p -(1-p) log2(1-p)`, describes model predictive uncertainty under that model; rare deterministic outcomes have low entropy even if almost no evidence has been collected. Expected information gain for an annotation is `H(theta|D) - E_review[H(theta|D, review)]`, conditional on a defined review outcome, cost, annotator accuracy and model. Use it to prioritize a reviewed sample only after checking that reviewers and observation probabilities are calibrated; rare languages, uncertain source identity and equity of coverage also matter, so a gain-only queue can worsen existing bias.

## Linguistic evidence and what it can identify

Record a separately reviewed sense relation (exact, narrower, broader, overlap, disjoint, unclear) between source sense and frozen concept, POS, context, native parameter and elicitation prompt, plus whether two senses derive from one original entry, translation or questionnaire item. High semantic overlap can make two slots near duplicates; do not treat that as a cognitive discovery. Do not impose WordNet distance or embeddings as an inclusion gate: unexpected links can be genuine, and relatedness may have a nonmonotonic relationship with colexification ([Brochhagen and Boleda 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2022.105179)). Separate reviewed homonym, polysemous lexical entry, underspecified broad sense and unresolved identity, with evidence, uncertainty and inter-annotator disagreement. A spelling/profile is not necessarily a phonemic form. Morpheme boundaries, tone, quantity, phonation, stress, stage, dialect and source conventions matter; preserve the source form and profile, do not pool inequivalent bases.

Concept-mapping reviews should sample positives *and* comparable nonmatches and unknown/held cases, stratified by source, basis, family, region and predicted risk, not just conspicuous graph edges. Record probability of selection and independent double reviews/adjudication to estimate source-specific precision and detect selection bias. Sample sources and languages including South America, isolates, small families and written historical stages; report sample counts and provenance. Record original questionnaire availability and source ancestry (original fieldwork, compilation, derivative, shared lexical material), not merely file namespaces. Loan and cognacy flags are helpful evidence where reviewed, but missing flags are not no-loan or no-cognacy labels. Same source or separate resource is not equivalent to dependent or independent field observations.

Observed identity `Y` can be modeled as an imperfect measurement of a proposed latent state `Z`, for example `P(Y=1|Z=1,A=1)=sensitivity` and `P(Y=1|Z=0,A=1)=1-specificity`. However, neither sensitivity nor specificity nor prevalence is jointly identifiable from one imperfect observation per case: an observed positive rate 0.2 is compatible with true prevalence 0.2 and perfect measurement, or prevalence 0.1 with a suitable false-positive rate. A reviewed validation set or repeated conditionally independent measurements with defensible constraints is required. Repeated derivative dictionaries are not independent replicates. A latent binary state must also specify *what* is true, for which speaker, stage, sense and lexeme; there may be genuine variation rather than one error-free universal label. Model ascertainment `P(A=1|Z,X)` separately only with questionnaire/availability evidence and explicit assumptions. Rubin's ignorability conditions are substantive, not automatically satisfied ([Rubin 1976](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/63.3.581)); nonignorable missingness generally needs unidentified sensitivity parameters or external data ([Linero and Daniels 2018](https://doi.org/10.1214/17-STS630)). Vary assumed undocumented-positive odds over a declared range; do not claim the data estimated that range.

## Dependence and stronger future model

Family random intercepts partially pool correlations but do not represent branching, borrowings or source genealogy. For a curated subset with dated family trees, stage alignment, coordinates and original-source relationships, consider a hierarchical latent-state phylogenetic model with tree uncertainty, family-specific transition rates, a sparse contact/areal term and source measurement layer. Fit within families first; compare tree-only, space-only, source-only and combined models on held-out families and areas. Phylogenetic methods have been applied to language evolution ([Dunn et al. 2011](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09923)); that does not validate any particular tree, diffusion process or worldwide exchangeability. Contact and inheritance are often statistically confounded. Use literature and independently dated evidence to constrain them and state when a parameter is not identified. Avoid fitting a global model with dozens of per-pair effects and sparse labels merely because it can be written down.

## Validation and computational protocol

Pre-register fixed pair selection and objective; include common, sparse, high-coverage, surprising, broad-slot and low-resource cases, including nonedges chosen independently of observed results. Reserve a genuinely unseen original-resource lineage as an external test, and separate family/areal holdouts from ordinary source holdout; a source can span many families and a family many sources, so enforce connected components of the known dependence graph where feasible and explicitly report unavoidable overlap. Train-only preprocessing and selection avoid leakage. Test coverage by family, region, basis and source; report exact exclusions, complete denominators and uncertainty with family/resource block resampling as sensitivity, not a guaranteed confidence interval under unknown dependence. If holdouts leave no train/test support, say the experiment is not identifiable rather than relaxing silently. CLICS overlap is descriptive comparison, not independent validation when upstream data overlap.

Use held-out log loss, Brier score, reliability diagrams, calibration intercept/slope where sample size supports them, precision/recall and rates by stratum. Check model versus pair-only, coverage-only, empirical family-balanced and current-score baselines; report performance against zero-gain and paired block uncertainty. Simulation must include known broad-slot expansions, source copying, inherited clusters, uneven coverage, coarse notation, homophony, misaligned senses and family/resource confounding, with null and alternative scenarios. Test recovery, false positives, calibration, prior sensitivity and shifts after withholding whole blocks. Independent expert reviews of sampled positives and nonmatches assess measurement; evaluation labels must not reuse model-training annotations. Performance under generated assumptions is not proof of real-world calibration.

Implement the pilot as a separate disk-backed, snapshot-hashed analysis: stream eligible scope/pair rows from SQLite, materialize only preselected pairs, retain indexes and audit counts; never allocate the full language × all-concept-pair matrix. Sequential bounded workers, small minibatches, SQLite temp on disk, deterministic seeds, explicit time/disk/address-space limits and fail-closed output preserve the 256 MiB worker constraint. An incidence-null pilot can run one scope and one chain at a time with bounded sparse adjacency and streaming statistics; first measure feasibility and mixing. Fit simple penalized models with bounded design matrices or streamed sufficient statistics; a future MCMC/phylogenetic program is a separately benchmarked research environment, not promised to fit 256 MiB. Preserve build IDs, input/code hashes, scope rules, software, priors, random seeds, group assignments and exact split lists. No full rebuild is needed to decide whether the pilot is worthwhile.

## Decision gates

1. Audit available schema, coverage and eligible labels; verify no leakage, and freeze a stratified pair/sample protocol.
2. Independently annotate a probability sample including negatives and provenance; establish whether enough reliable test cases and independent source blocks exist.
3. Run small descriptive prediction and null pilots; compare baselines and report calibration and dependence sensitivities. If coverage, design or predictive performance is inadequate, stop at descriptive statistics.
4. Only then consider published probabilities, graph weights or latent-state inference, with explicit separate review. A predictive improvement demonstrates useful information in this collection under a stated split, not a universal conceptual category or causal history.

## Addendum: stress test of the proposed pilot

**Prediction is not validation.** A model can predict erroneous records exceptionally well: a headword-mapping error repeated across derivative resources is predictable precisely because it is copied. Treat predictive performance as evidence about recorded identity alone. Separately draw a probability sample of predicted positives, observed positives, nonmatches and held/unknown cases, blinded to model score where feasible; independent reviewers check original senses, source relationship and comparison representation against a prespecified target. Report weighted precision for observed positives and, where an independently reviewed comparable inventory permits it, sensitivity and specificity. Double-review a subset and publish disagreements and selection weights. Reviewers must not use the prediction or downstream graph as their ground truth. A genuine validity evaluation requires original evidence capable of settling the relevant lexical question: otherwise label the review unresolved, rather than count it correct or incorrect. Prediction scores can prioritize review but cannot certify it.

**A disconnected joint holdout may not exist.** Constructing a bipartite graph of families and resource lineages, an edge representing any observation, often produces a giant connected component. Entire-component splitting then has few independent units or no feasible train/test partition. Do not sever the component and claim joint independence. Instead report distinct explicitly conditional targets: (a) leave-one-source-lineage-out prediction for new sources, acknowledging familiar families and shared fieldwork not represented in the lineage graph; (b) leave-one-family-out prediction for new families, acknowledging familiar resources and shared source processes; (c) leave-one-area-out as an additional geographic transfer stress test, acknowledging geographic and source overlap; (d) a genuinely external prospective source with documented independent collection and family/area overlap disclosed. Repeated group splits and uncertainty summaries describe variation among available groups, not a general guarantee for a population of families or sources. When effective group counts are too small, report per-group outcomes and suppress pooled uncertainty claims. A small manually chosen unconnected evaluation set can be informative, but is selective and cannot represent the whole database without a sampling argument. A dual-holdout analysis is valid for a conditional subset only if actual independence assumptions are defensible and exclusions are disclosed; its narrower target is not worldwide performance.

**Collinearity is a design limit.** In `logit(p)=alpha+u_family+v_resource`, if all records of one family occur in one resource and that resource records only that family, their contributions enter only as `u+v`. The likelihood cannot identify the separate components: distinct prior scales can produce distinct allocations with nearly identical predictions. Crossed family-resource observations may improve separation, but only if connected, sufficiently replicated and not confounded with coverage and representation. Show the family-resource incidence graph, covariate overlap and likelihood/profile or posterior correlations; fit source-only, family-only and combined models and repeat under materially different prior scales. Predictive robustness is not evidence that the two variance components are independently learned. If designs do not support separation, use a single observable group effect or simpler pooled baseline and refrain from attributing error or linguistic variation to either source or ancestry.

**Bounded uncertainty is still possible.** For a defined finite frame of `N` accepted scopes, let `n1` be observed positives, `n0` observed comparable nonmatches and `u=N-n1-n0` unknown. If the target is the fraction of scopes that *would yield an eligible recorded identity under a fixed, fully specified completion protocol*, and observed outcomes are accepted as accurate for that protocol, its worst-case identification region is `[n1/N, (n1+u)/N]`. For example, 20 positive, 80 comparable nonmatching and 100 unknown scopes yield `[0.10, 0.60]`; the observed comparable fraction 0.20 is only one possible completion. This is a finite-frame bound, not a confidence interval and not a bound on latent linguistic prevalence unless much stronger assumptions connect the measurement to linguistic truth. Provide subgroup bounds by source, family, region and basis and retain exclusions; an undefined protocol cannot support this estimand. An interpretable sensitivity parameter `q=P(Y=1 | A=0)` yields completed fraction `(n1+u q)/N` under an explicitly assumed common missing-scope rate, varied over a justified range; strata can use separate q values, and none is learned merely by fitting the observed sample. If selected scopes and validated labels result from a known probability-sampling design, design-based confidence intervals for the sampled observed-frame rate and sampling uncertainty around bounds are possible; exact binomial intervals require independent Bernoulli sampling and are generally unjustified for this convenience corpus. A Bayesian sensitivity interval may integrate uncertainty in specified `q` and error rates, but is conditional on those priors and cannot be advertised as design-based coverage. If measured classification is uncertain, further widen bounds using independently reviewed error-rate uncertainty and propagate the validation sampling design. Avoid population-level confidence claims without a defensible sampling frame.

**The incidence null is not accidental homophony.** Swapping concept labels across existing identity groups while preserving group degrees and concept degrees holds the observed distribution of lexical ambiguity (how many concepts share an identity) fixed. Its null asks whether *this particular pair's co-membership* is unusual under exchangeable assignments conditional on those margins, scope and structural constraints. It cannot estimate how many observed identities are chance homophones, because those identities and ambiguity counts are inputs held fixed, not outcomes generated by the null. Likewise rejecting that null establishes neither polysemy nor cognitive association. A separate form-generating collision model must specify the inventory, form probabilities, phonotactics, length, representation and dependence, and requires validation against independently annotated homonyms; even that distinguishes accidental collisions only under its generative assumptions.
